Where to Surf in Santa Cruz: Your Guide to Riding Waves in California’s Original Surf City


I grew up thinking I lived in “Surf City.” And I did. Being born and raised in Santa Cruz, my childhood was defined by long days spent surfing, skimboarding, and boogie-boarding at Sunny Cove, Seabright, and 26th. It was a lifestyle that produced an infinite supply of sand stored in the cracks of my mom’s car. Showing up to class at Harbor High with salty surf hair and weekends working the register at Arrow Surf Shop was the norm. 

However, in 2006 our little surf community was blindsided by the city of Huntington Beach claiming the trademark “Surf City USA” for themselves. So, technically, Santa Cruzans can no longer claim the official “Surf City,” but everyone knows that the true Surf City is here in the Monterey Bay. After all, there’s a reason why the Beach Boys mentioned Santa Cruz – not Huntington – in their 1963 hit “Surfin’ U.S.A.”

Let’s not forget the first waves ever ridden on a surfboard in North America were here in Santa Cruz. Hawaiian princes visiting California rode redwood carved boards at the mouth of the San Lorenzo River in 1885. Thus, surfing is forever imbued in our town’s history, character, and chilly water. Huntington Beach can keep its title as well as its endless concrete, pay-to-park beach lots, and soulless shopping malls.

I’ve been lucky enough to travel the world for surfing – visiting some of the most renowned destinations like Indonesia, Costa Rica, Fiji, and, most recently, Tahiti to cover the 2024 Olympics. While I do enjoy surfing in my trunks in tropical water, I always love coming home and returning to the beautiful point breaks that raised me here in Santa Cruz.

Santa Cruz is littered with great surf spots along the county’s coast. There is something for everyone to ride, from beginners to experts. Here are 14 spots (among many more!) to consider while you look for waves to ride around town.
Eastside
Pleasure Point

Surfing on Santa Cruz’s Eastside is centered around Pleasure Point – or simply “the Point” as locals often call it. Back in my Harbor High surf team days, we would show up at take over the point after school on Tuesdays, rain or shine, big or small.

Pleasure Point is a magnet for swell energy, so there is almost always something to surf when other spots in town are flat. The point consists of three distinct right-hand waves: Sewers, First Peak, and Second Peak. Sewers is a shallow wave that breaks fast and hollow at the top of the point. Next, there is First Peak, which features a much longer ride than Sewers. Then at the bottom of the point, there is Second Peak, a more mellow wave better suited for longboarders and intermediate surfers. 

Pleasure Point has many different faces according to the conditions, but from one foot to 10 feet, you can almost always find something fun to ride among the three peaks. As a result, it’s also one of the most crowded spots in town, but that’s part of surfing in our little slice of surf paradise.

    Skill level: Intermediate to expert
    Parking: Free parking in the surrounding neighborhood
    Access: For First Peak and Second Peak, there are staircases at 33rd and 36th; for Sewers, there is a staircase/small rock jump at Pleasure Point Drive
    Bathrooms/showers: Yes, at the intersection of East Cliff Drive and Pleasure Point Drive
